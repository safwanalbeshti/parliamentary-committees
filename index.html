Parliamentary Committees, in Plain English

There are 3 types of parliamentary committee in the UK:

1

Select

These are long-term committees that investigate and scrutinise subjects/departments/policies, via:

  • Holding private meetings (where they choose inquiries, discuss evidence, and make decisions).
  • Holding public oral-evidence sessions (where they question ministers, officials, experts, etc.) Click me
  • Receiving written evidence (from organisations / individuals who have been invited to write).
  • Making reports/recommendations (after deliberating privately) which the government often responds to.
  • Correspondence (ie. having back-and-forth exchanges with ministers/departments/companies/regulators).
  • Fact-finding visits, public engagement (eg. surveys).
2

General

These are commons-only committees set up to deal with a specific item of business/legislation, with membership appointed for that particular task.

This is an exhaustive list of the different types of general committees:

  • Public Bill Committees (examine Bills line by line)
  • Delegated Legislation Committees
  • Second Reading Committees
  • Committees to consider Bills on Report
  • Commons Grand Committees
    • Scottish Grand Committee
    • Welsh Grand Committee
    • Northern Ireland Grand Committee
  • Regional Affairs Committee
  • European Committees
3

Lords Grand Committee

This is an alternative debating forum for the House of Lords where Bills are scrutinised in detail line-by-line (but no formal votes are taken — decisions must be unanimous).

What this is

Parliamentary committees publish full transcripts of their evidence sessions, but they are long and formal. This page retells one session as a short dialogue you can step through at your own pace.

Wording is simplified, exchanges are condensed, and several committee members’ questions may be merged into one voice — nothing here is a direct quote. For the verbatim record, follow the official transcript link: official transcript ↗.
