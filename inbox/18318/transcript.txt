If we have been out for whatever reason, because ultimately the IDB has decided to do something else, then it causes major headaches for district councils, my own and particularly those authorities that have a much higher levy that they have to pay.

We recognise the role that the IDBs do. They perform an important function. Accountability is important, too, and we have the opportunity—and we certainly take that up—to have representatives from the district councils on the boards. Generally, that helps to prevent the shocks. Ultimately, though, I do not think this is sustainable in the long run. We are not seeing a situation in which there is more money coming in to local authorities to account for the additional requests that IDBs are placing on it. Ultimately, it means that local authorities are having to make cuts elsewhere in order to fund the IDBs. Without additional money coming into the system, it is local authorities and particularly district councils that are left with the financial problem in a sector that has, frankly, had enough financial headaches over the last few years.
