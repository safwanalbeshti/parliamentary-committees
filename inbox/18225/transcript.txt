Q1 Chair: Welcome to this session of the Public Accounts Committee on Thursday 29 October 2026. Child benefit supports around 6.9 million families and 11.7 million children. With £13.3 billion paid out in 2024-25, this makes it one of the largest individual benefits that HMRC pays out. HMRC must protect public money from fraud and error while ensuring that eligible families receive the support on which they rely.

In 2025, HMRC began using Home Office travel data to identify claimants who might have moved overseas and no longer be eligible for child benefit. While this was an innovative attempt to address the estimated £270 million in fraud and error, the roll-out of this intervention was poorly handled and led to avoidable hardship for thousands of legitimate claimants. More than 8,000 eligible claimants had their payments suspended without warning, while many faced a difficult and stressful process to prove their legitimate entitlement.

Today the Committee will examine why HMRC altered the approach used in its pilot without adequately assessing the consequences, whether its governance and safeguards are now strong enough, and how the Government can use data innovatively to tackle fraud and error while protecting payments to legitimate claimants.

As you say, child benefit is an essential benefit for millions of families in this country, and we are stepping through a process of transforming the experience to make it much more digital and easier to access and stay in touch with us through our app. That is working well, but the error and fraud level is too high. We need to bring it down. We are trying to use innovative approaches to target that on a small number of customers for whom we think that is a higher level of risk. That included, as you say, the Home Office data to identify those who may have left the country but are still claiming.

The pilot was well intended. It was meant to prove the concepts, and I am confident that the exercise has now settled down well in delivery. We can talk about both those points and how I derive that assurance, but I acknowledge that, in the first roll-out phase, we made some mistakes. Q50 Chair: I will move on now to the slightly wider experience and talk about the lessons that HMRC have learned from this issue. This is for Rob Malcomson. What was the experience here and how have the lessons been applied across wider Government?

Q51 Chair: Is there anything more that could be done? Are there any Departments that are not listening to you?
