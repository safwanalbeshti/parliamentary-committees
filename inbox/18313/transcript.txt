Backbench Business Committee

Representations: Backbench Debates

Tuesday 17 November 2026

Ordered by the House of Commons to be published on 17 November 2026.

Watch the meeting

Members present: Martin Vickers; Mr Lee Dillon; Alison Hume; Will Stone.

Questions 1-6

Representations made

I: Grahame Morris

II: Alison Hume

Grahame Morris made representations.

Chair: Good afternoon and welcome to this meeting of the Backbench Business Committee. We will first consider the application from Grahame Morris. Grahame, over to you.

Grahame Morris: Thanks very much, Chair, and thanks to the Committee for hearing my application. Can I reassure the Committee that the debate I am speaking in support of is not about the future of the Backbench Business Committee, but about the future of the British Broadcasting Corporation. If the Committee would indulge me, and if it is possible, I am asking for time before Christmas because we are expecting the Government’s long-awaited White Paper later this year, before Christmas.

The BBC is already making decisions about jobs and programmes. Members may have heard about cuts to particular channels—BBC Scotland and news channels and so on—which will shape the service that is shaped by the new charter. We feel that Back-Bench MPs should have the opportunity to put their constituents’ views on the record and ask Ministers how they propose to respond.

In my own constituency, Easington in the north-east, BBC Radio Newcastle and “Look North” are practical examples of what our public service broadcasting means for constituents. Colleagues from across the UK have got their own examples of why local radio and regional news and programming matter, especially to people who rely on broadcast services, particularly the elderly and housebound.

The five unions supporting the broadcasting, entertainment and arts unions all-party parliamentary group, of which I am an officer, represent people who report, write, perform, record and produce BBC output. Those trade unions want urgent action on cuts, a sustainable public service, a funding settlement, secure and separate funding for the World Service, and a formal voice for workers on an editorially independent BBC board.

Cuts to staff and commissioning affect the quality and range of programmes that audiences can expect, as well as threatening jobs and freelance work across the UK. A debate would allow Members to test the Government’s approach to those choices and ask what can be done before the next charter takes effect. We would welcome time before Christmas, if the Committee were able to provide it, to debate this in a 90-minute debate.

Will Stone: Thank you for bringing forward this important debate application. For a Chamber debate, we need a minimum of 15 Member names. Would you be happy to go away and get four additional names, or would you be content with a Westminster Hall debate?

Grahame Morris: The most important thing is that we have the debate before Christmas. I do not know what time slots are available, but I am fairly confident in the application that was submitted. It followed quite quickly on the heels of the annual meeting of the broadcasting, entertainment and arts unions APPG. We did not have a lot of time, but there is also an APPG for freelancers, and I am sure that members of that group would support it. Jo Stevens has indicated that she would support it, although she is not a signatory.

Of course, the DCMS Committee did a report on BBC charter renewal, and we only have Rupa Huq as a signatory from that Committee. I have spoken to several Members, from across parties, who are interested and have indicated that they would welcome the opportunity to speak in such a debate. The short answer is yes, I am fairly confident that we could get more names, but if you cannot spare the time in the Chamber before Christmas, we would take a 90-minute debate in Westminster Hall. But our first choice would be the Chamber, if that is possible.

Alison Hume: I should advise Members that I sit on the DCMS Committee. Thank you for bringing this application. I am concerned about the fact that the only live programme made for BBC Radio 4 outside London, “You and Yours”, is being axed. I have many constituents who value that specialist consumer program. That is just one of the cuts. I support your application and I hope that we can put it in, in a timely manner.

Chair: The Clerks will be in touch with you, Grahame. If the debate is held on a Tuesday morning, you appreciate that it must be a morning when that Department is replying.

Grahame Morris: I understand.

Chair: Thank you.

Alison Hume made representations.

Chair: Our next applicant is Alison Hume. Over to you, Alison.

Alison Hume: Thank you for inviting me to present my application for a Backbench Business debate on priorities for the Government's next national autism strategy.

That subject is important because autistic people continue to face some of the most extreme health inequalities in society. Did you know, Chair, that autistic people die between 6 and 15 years earlier than the general population? Only 26% of autistic students say that they feel happy at school, and only three in 10 autistic adults are in work—that figure is among the lowest for any disability.

The Autism Act 2009 required the Government to produce and regularly update a national autism strategy. However, the House of Lords, which has done amazing work on this, found that, despite those efforts, outcomes have not improved. The current strategy expired at the end of June 2026. The Government have said that they are committed to publishing a new strategy, and that the lifespan of the current strategy will be extended while a new one is developed.

The reason for this application is that a debate would be a timely opportunity for Members of Parliament from all parties to engage with the next autism strategy while it is being developed. That is important, as the Government are rolling out the new SEND offer. My application is well supported, across parties. I would be very grateful if the Committee considered my application for a Westminster Hall debate.

Mr Dillon: Those are quite shocking numbers, so I can see the reason for requesting a debate. Is there a particular date you would like it to coincide with, or are you happy for it to be scheduled in the next available slot?

Alison Hume: I would be very happy for it to be scheduled in the next available slot.

Chair: I presume the responding Department would be Health.

Alison Hume: Yes.

Chair: In that case—[Interruption.] Oh, you have asked for a Thursday anyway, so the Tuesday restriction does not apply—very good. As a member of the Committee, I think you know that the Clerks will be in touch.

Alison Hume: Thank you.

Chair: That concludes the public section of our meeting. We will now go into private session to consider the applications.
