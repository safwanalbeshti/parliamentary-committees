Chair: Good morning—or guten Morgen—to our guests. Today, the UK Foreign Affairs Committee is holding its first joint session with our sister Committee from the German Bundestag. We have visited you, and we are so pleased that you accepted the invitation to come back for this joint session.

This session is against the background of the Kensington Treaty, which sets out co-operation between the UK and Germany and was formally ratified last week when the Prime Minister visited the German Chancellor. Both our Committees will hold our Governments to account when it comes to delivering on that treaty, but it is also important for parliamentarians to come together to discuss our priorities and understand where we have concerns or where we can point out best practice to ensure that our relationship is as good as it can be. We all have a role to play in that. We want to have a closer relationship with our European partners. Germany is and always has been a key ally in so many areas. We want to talk about many of those today.

Armin Laschet: Thank you very much again for the invitation. It is an important time to exchange views, even on the parliamentarian level, because Parliaments have different political parties and different political positions.

Therefore, it is good to discuss not only in your own country, but with important partners. That was the idea of the Kensington Treaty: to not only bring Governments together in different fields, but to have a parliamentarian dimension. Therefore, we are happy to discuss Ukraine, the economy, competitiveness between the European Union and Great Britain and the future of our relations.
