Marie Goldman and Dr Kieran Mullan made representations.

Chair: Welcome to this meeting of the Backbench Business Committee, in which we will be considering applications from colleagues for debates in the Chamber and in Westminster Hall on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The first application is from Marie Goldman and for a debate on protection for homeowners targeted by Asset Invest, and the request is for a Tuesday Westminster Hall slot. Marie, please present your case.

Marie Goldman: Thank you very much, Chair. I appreciate the opportunity to apply for the debate.

Since the beginning of the year, my team in my Chelmsford constituency have been contacted by a number of residents who have been targeted by a company called Asset Invest, who have bought the restrictive covenants in the title deeds to their properties—this affects a lot of properties in my constituency—and are trying to extort, in some cases, tens of thousands of pounds from my constituents. I have examples of some residents who have paid over £20,000, because this company target residents particularly when they see on Rightmove that the property is for sale. That might be because the homeowner themselves wants to move; sometimes people are in the middle of probate. At risk of losing the sale, they feel compelled to hand over the money.

This is having a really distressing impact on my constituents. Although we have tried to raise awareness in my constituency, it is very difficult to get the word out, and people come across this only when they are about to sell their property and the company are using pressure selling tactics. I held a town hall meeting with constituents—several town hall meetings, actually. At least 200 constituents have come along. The issue affects a large number of people, but it is not just in my Chelmsford constituency. I am here with the hon. Member for Bexhill and Battle. We are working cross-party because this issue also affects people in his constituency, as he will explain in a minute, and people right across the country.

With the draft Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill to come before Parliament, we think this is a really good time to bring this issue to other Members’ attention and to do something about it. We have the opportunity. I think it is incumbent on us to do something about it to protect constituents right across the country.

Dr Mullan: Could I add some numbers to that? We have at least 15 MPs whose constituents have been targeted in this way, and people are paying out significant amounts of money: a constituent in the Gosport constituency paid £11,300, one in the Stafford constituency paid £7,500, and one in Fareham and Waterlooville paid more than £10,000. We know that the company have restrictive covenants in titles in 41 other constituencies, so this issue goes across the country, affecting potentially thousands of homes. This is about just one home builder; there could be others.

We have already met representatives of surveyors, lenders and solicitors, and all these key stakeholders recognise that this is a problem that needs tackling. It has received national attention, having been featured twice on “You and Yours” as they have tried to highlight these cases. As Marie said, a debate is the ideal opportunity for us to lay out the background ahead of further discussions, we hope, around the leasehold reform Bill.

Mary Glindon: The Committee asks for an equal split between Government and Opposition Back Benchers, but on your application there is only one Government Back Bencher. Are you able to find three more to support it?

Dr Mullan: The challenge has been that Government MPs are exactly that. They have positions, they are PPSs, and so on, which makes it difficult for them to put their names to the application. Several Labour MPs are affected; whether you happen to get enough names is just a question of speed. We can certainly send you a list of the Government MPs whose constituencies are affected by this.

Mary Glindon: Can you approach them to ask—

Dr Mullan: Does the application have to name them specifically in order for it to be accepted?

Mary Glindon: Yes.

Dr Mullan: Okay.

Marie Goldman: We will approach them.

Mary Glindon: Thank you. Good luck.

Mr Dillon: Did you say that there are 15 MPs and then another 41 seats, so it is 56 constituencies in total?

Dr Mullan: At least, because as I said, we have only one house builder that they purchase from. By the way, they bought their entitlement to do all this for £1. We have since found another house builder that we think they have also bought titles off, so it could be many more.

Marie Goldman: They are absolute chancers. That is what I am saying.

Chair: Generally with a restrictive covenant, someone has the benefit of the covenant, so how are they discovering who has the benefit of the covenant?

Dr Mullan: It is their covenant; they have purchased it, so officially they have the benefit of the covenant. Of course it’s ridiculous: they have no interest in maintaining the covenant and they actually never try to maintain the covenant. They never say, “We won’t grant you retrospective consent,” because, from their point of view, they are not the least bit interested in some random housing estate and whether someone has a conservatory. It is purely a method of extracting money.

As I understand it, the house builders originally had the covenants in place for the early part of the housing development and the sale of the houses to keep uniformity across the houses. That was more than 30 years ago. They are now, out of the blue, going to people who have planning permission for these developments—it is all legally done, and it all has planning permission. We quizzed them early on about how they are coming to these fees and the basis for saying it costs that much money, and they never gave us any detailed answers. They then stopped corresponding with me and the other MPs who were writing to them, and they have refused to correspond with us for several months.

Marie Goldman: It feels very much like they pluck a figure out of the air and see what they can get away with. Unfortunately, because of these pressure selling tactics, some people feel compelled to pay over the money simply so that their house sale does not fall through.

Chair: I would love to continue the discussion; it is quite an interesting subject. If you could kindly provide the extra names on the Government side, we will support the application. They need to be able to come and speak, not just support the application.

Dr Mullan: Speak in the actual debate?

Chair: Yes.

Dr Mullan: How many have to speak in the debate on the Government side?

Chair: We would expect an even division. It is eight people, so it would be four from the Government side and four from the Opposition. Kindly supply those and we can consider the application accordingly.

Dr Mullan: Okay.

Jim Shannon made representations.

Chair: The application from our season ticket holder is for a debate on protecting the rights of the next generation to freedom of religion or belief. This is an application for Westminster Hall on a Tuesday for a 90-minute debate. Is that correct?

Jim Shannon: I am happy if it is Tuesday or Thursday. I understand the pressures you are under.

Will Stone: That clears one question out of the way.

Jim Shannon: A few more names have been added: Lincoln Jopp and Ruth Jones.

I am the chair of the APPG for international freedom of religion or belief. It is a fundamental human right protected under article 18 of the universal declaration of human rights and article 14 of the UN convention on the rights of the child. This application is very specific; it is about freedom of religion or belief for the next generation. I want to put that on record.

Children and young people worldwide continue to face religious persecution, discrimination, forced conversions and restrictions on religion, religious expression and education. The UK Parliament has consistently demonstrated its commitment to FORB; that is not in doubt. The present Government have put a special envoy in place, so their commitment is there. Commitment has also been demonstrated through written questions and the work of the all-party parliamentary group for international freedom of religion or belief.

In 2023, Fiona Bruce, who was then the Member for Congleton and the Prime Minister’s special envoy, presented evidence to the Backbench Business Committee that highlighted educational initiatives to promote religious tolerance among children. More recently, a parliamentary debate on 17 July 2025—just over 15 or 16 months ago—on FORB in foreign policy reflected sustained cross-party engagement with this issue.

Parliamentary scrutiny has also specifically addressed children’s religious freedoms, including forced conversions. I am very concerned about them, especially in Pakistan, which we have been to a few times, and in other countries across the world. Parliamentary scrutiny has addressed forced conversions of minority girls, religious discrimination in schools and children’s independent rights to belief. These issues have been raised by parliamentarians including Lord Alton, me and you, Chair, in the roles you play outside this Committee. My request is for an hour and a half on a Tuesday or a Thursday.

Chair: I take it your question has been answered, Will.

Will Stone: I think he has covered it, yes.

Mr Dillon: Which Department would you like to respond to the debate?

Jim Shannon: Forgive me—I can name the Member, but I cannot remember his Department: Chris Elmore might be the Minister responsible for it. Stephen Doughty has the far east, which is where the religious conversions and kidnapping of young girls of school age is happening. I am not quite sure if it is his responsibility.

Mr Dillon: You think FCDO though?

Jim Shannon: Oh yes, it is definitely FCDO. I think it might be Chris Elmore.

Mr Dillon: Another one from me: the form that you submitted it on is out of date, so it does not have the question on there about which Department will answer. I think the Clerks have emailed you an updated form for future applications.

Jim Shannon: Perfect; that is grand. I am sure that I will be back again.

Chair: Can you delete your templates and use that one?

Jim Shannon: It’s the new, modern one for this old boy—okay, right.

Jonathan Davies: We had a question about the number of Government Back Benchers on the list, but you have explained that Ruth Jones has been added, so it is compliant now.

Jim Shannon: Yes, it is four Labour, three Conservatives, two Lib Dems, two independents and two DUP.

Chair: Thanks very much. The Clerks will be in touch with you in due course. If I can just say, the letter that is due to go to the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office about Bangladesh is now in the hands of Barry or Andrew. It has been passed on.

Jim Shannon: That is great. I will leave that with you, if that is okay. As always, Chair, thank you and the Committee for your kindness.

Ruth Jones made representations.

Chair: This is an application for a debate on the breeding of dogs and cats with exaggerated features. It is for a Westminster Hall debate on a Tuesday morning or a Thursday afternoon. Ruth, please present your case.

Ruth Jones: This is primarily an educational debate, because more and more cats and dogs are being bred with these exaggerated features. We are all familiar with boxers and their difficulty breathing, but more and more dogs are being bred with flat faces, floppy ears or screw tails. Everybody thinks they are very cute—handbag dogs, and things like that—but in fact, people are breeding in pain and deformity, and shortening the lives of animals.

We know what social media is like: you see a cute little dog with a pop star—I hesitate to use the names of pop stars, because the ones I know are all dead, so it is not going to help—which, in the modern day, is a trend. There is a worry about pester power, with people saying, “Mum and Dad, please can we have one of these cute little folded-ears cats?”, but they do not realise that the cat actually has deformities in all its joints. It is all about education really.

I believe that the debate would be well subscribed; obviously, our animal welfare colleagues are keen to speak in it. It is all about highlighting the issues, because places like rescue centres are spending a lot of time and money operating on these animals to keep them alive, when in fact they should be spending money on other things.

Alison Hume: Thank you for this application. I was listening to something on the radio that said, unfortunately, focusing on the problems that the animals suffer has backfired previously because it has led to people having rescue motivations—“I’ll have those and I’ll make them better”—but of course the problem is with the breeding. It is very interesting. Is there any time sensitivity to the debate, or any day you would like it to be on or near?

Ruth Jones: No, there is nothing in particular. It is just a question of highlighting the issue to the general public. As you say, lots of people go, “Oh, they’re cute. We must rescue them,” but then they find that they cannot cope with them or they cannot afford £6,000 for an operation, so the animals are then dumped. It is all about making sure that we educate the public.

Chair: Thank you very much, Ruth. The Clerks will be in touch with you in due course.

Ruth Jones: Thank you very much.

Chair: That concludes the public business of the Committee. The Committee will now go into private session to consider the bids and allocate time accordingly.
