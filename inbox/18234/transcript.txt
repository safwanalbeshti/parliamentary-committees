Chair: Such as?

Chair: Indeed. Let’s start with the devolution by default approach. As accounting officer, do you see this as an opportunity or as a major risk? If the latter, is it on your risk register? How do you see it, and how are you going to mitigate those risks?

Dame Sarah Healey: I come at this with a really unique perspective from my previous role as permanent secretary at the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government.

Chair: That is helpful, isn’t it?

Dame Sarah Healey: Yes, it is very helpful—well, I hope it is going to be helpful—as DWP navigates that scale of ambition. I was responsible for lots of conversations with my fellow accounting officers across Government, Peter included, as we set up the integrated settlement and thought about the ways in which we could preserve accounting officer accountability in a devolved world. Obviously, DWP has committed to devolving more, but over the course of the spending review, it has already devolved £3.1 billion of employment support and other funding as part of the integrated settlement. I am very familiar with the mechanisms that are currently in place for the integrated settlement, but it is very clear that those are going to have to transform and change over the course of the next few years, not least because of two things.

The first is the commitments set out in the “Rewiring the State” blueprint just after the Prime Minister came into office—the ambition to have devolution by default and the desire to have very proportionate accountability systems that may involve local accounting officers and scrutiny processes that respond to that. Secondly, and critically, there is fiscal devolution. You will have seen the plan for fiscal devolution set out alongside the Budget, which I think will fundamentally change the way that mayoral strategic authorities receive their funding, rather than through grants from Departments—it is a very different way of doing it. That will require different accountability arrangements.

I also ought to say that I think it is possible to have a significant amount of devolution of responsibility to local areas and still maintain accounting officer responsibility to Parliament. If you look at how that is done in the local government system, for instance, as the accounting officer in MHCLG, I had responsibility for the overall local authority system and spending. I was not individually responsible for the spending and performance of individual local authorities, and I think there is a lot to learn about how that system operates—Catherine probably knows it better than I do, frankly, having been an accountable officer in local government. I think there is a lot to learn from that about how we can get the real benefits of allowing local decision making and local design of services while ensuring there is proper accountability for taxpayers’ money alongside that.

Chair: Do you think devolution of the budget for employment support will mean that we have greater uptake, because we have not had a very good response? We have had 190,000, which is considerably below the target for getting young people, and older people, into work.

Dame Sarah Healey: What I heard from mayors up and down the country in my previous job, speaking to them about what they see as the benefits of having greater ownership of these budgets at regional and MSA level, is that they can link up those budgets with other forms of support and that they have very strong relationships with employers.

That is obviously building on fantastic stuff that happens in our jobcentres, which have really strong links with the communities they serve, but they see the mayoral perspective as vital to ensuring that people, especially people with a range of vulnerabilities, can have services wrapped around their needs at a local level. They see those as some of the benefits. You can see that in some of the pilot projects that have been done in Manchester, including the prevention trailblazer, to which we have obviously been contributing. With any approach that is about testing and innovation, you will get a variation in outcomes. We can expect to see that.

Dame Sarah Healey: Again, I really do not want you to think that I am in any way thinking that there is not more that we can do. Obviously, we want the vast majority of our staff to feel that change is managed well, but I was looking at those results only yesterday, in preparation for coming to the Committee, and I reflected that in comparison with the rest of Whitehall and most of the other Departments across Government, we are doing rather well on managing change.

It is a very difficult score to increase, in my experience as a permanent secretary. Even at DCMS—where I was the permanent secretary for three and a half years, and which is a much smaller Department that does not deliver services and therefore does not experience the scale of change that the DWP does—it was really hard to get that score over 50%. This was a Department that had the highest engagement score in Whitehall. Overall, people were very engaged by the Department and loved working there, but that particular measure is really hard to get up, because people do find it difficult to deal with change. They do find change hard.

I am not suggesting that we should not be trying to push that score up, but, considering the scale of what the DWP does, I think the rest of Whitehall has something to learn from us about how we have done it in the past and what it has involved. When you are changing something, what people really want to understand is that you are changing it, why you are changing it, whether they are being involved in the decision about what the change will be and whether they are being involved in the decision about how you are going to roll it out. I have always said that one of the things I prioritise most in the Departments that I run is trying to create a culture in which people feel safe to challenge. That is one of the closest correlations with the overall level of engagement that people have with the organisation that they work for. Catherine Vaughan: As Sarah mentioned, I come with a particular perspective, having spent more than a decade as a chief finance officer in local government. Dame Sarah Healey: It is obviously right that we should attempt to be as accurate as possible, that we do not see some of those errors of the past repeated and that we get as much right as possible first time.
