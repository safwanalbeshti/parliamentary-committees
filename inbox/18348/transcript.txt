Chair: Welcome to this session of the Public Accounts Committee on Monday 23 November 2026. Today we are examining the Government’s progress on boosting construction skills. From building new hospitals to improving the energy efficiency of homes, the Government’s ambitions depend on having enough workers with the right skills. This Committee has long highlighted the importance of developing the right skills to deliver economic growth and infrastructure.

In March 2025, the Government announced an initial £625 million package aimed at supporting up to 60,000 additional skilled construction workers by 2029. The National Audit Office’s July Report welcomed the mix of initiatives, but it warned that significant delivery risks remain. Success will depend not just on providing training places, but on engaging employers so that there is a path for people to move into construction jobs. The Government’s September announcement of new vocational GCSEs in England adds to this wider ambition, alongside T-levels.

We want to understand how Departments are ensuring enough high-quality placements and clear routes into apprenticeships and skilled employment, so that these qualifications can help to meet the sector’s needs. Today, we will examine whether Departments understand the skills required, how they are securing employers’ involvement, in particular, and whether training is translating into additional skilled construction workers where they are needed.

To help us with all that, we are pleased to have in front of us a very senior cast of witnesses: Dame Sarah Healey, DWP permanent secretary; Sinead O’Sullivan, director general for the skills group at the DfE; Alexandra Fitzpatrick, director for the joint skills strategy and young people at the DWP; and Sarah Maclean, chief executive officer of Skills England.
