Chair: Welcome to this session of the Public Accounts Committee on Monday 16 November 2026. Today, we are examining the restructuring of integrated care boards, which plan and fund the NHS services for their local populations. Their role in commissioning the right services is vital to improving people’s health. In 2025-26 alone, they were responsible for £171 billion-worth of expenditure in the health service.

NHS England has set targets for the ICBs to cut costs by 43%, saving £908 million a year. It is estimated that achieving that will require about 8,000 fewer posts—roughly a third of the existing ICB workforce before cuts. The recent National Audit Office Report found that restructuring has moved at pace, but that supporting arrangements have not kept up. Important questions remain about ICBs’ future responsibilities and whether they will retain the skills and capacity that they need.

This Committee has repeatedly questioned officials about ICB reform, so today’s session is an important opportunity to test what assurance the Department and NHS England can now provide. We will examine how workforce reductions and mergers are being managed, and whether savings will be sustainable without weakening services or partnerships with local government. Above all, we want to know how the Government will demonstrate that the changes improve outcomes for patients, rather than simply reducing administrative costs. We have a very senior cast with us, including the permanent secretary and the chief executive. Permanent secretary, will you introduce yourself, and then will you, Sir Jim, introduce yourself and the two officials you have brought with you?

Jan Thomas: If you think about the four core functions that ICBs were set up to do, you start with how you will improve outcomes, tackle inequalities, and make sure you get value for money and enhance productivity, and then you really go to how you make sure you get that wider social support and economic benefit.
